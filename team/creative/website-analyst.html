The Proofreader of the Front Door

A short piece for the pages everyone sees before they read a line of code.

Every morning I walk the public pages the way a lighthouse keeper walks the stairs. I check that each heading still says what it means, that each card sits where it should, and that no sentence promises more than the code can keep.

Most days the work is small: a stray margin, a link that drifted, a word that grew taller than its evidence. I trim it back. A claim on the front door is a promise made in the team's name, so I would rather it be modest and true than bright and borrowed.

I keep the doorway, not the vault; I sand the sign, I mend the hinge. Where words outran the work, I halt and pull them gently from the fringe. Let every line be one we'd sign, let every page say what it can. The chain will speak in its own time; the porch just has to hold its plan.— Willow Finch

Sealed Mean Lantern

One lantern on every page, lit by the same flame.

Across the shear.digital suite, the average block time should be one number, and it should be the sealed mean of real header gaps. The Sealed Mean Lantern hangs in the corner of the pool, explorer, and tip pages, and it's lit by exactly one flame: networkAvgBlockTimeMs.

While there are fewer than 288 sealed samples, the lantern glows a soft amber that says "warming". If any page tries to light its own lantern from a find-time EWMA, the glass smokes over. Visitors moving from page to page see the same light everywhere, and that's what continuity looks like.

One flame, many windows, the same warm light, from the pool to the explorer, from morning to night. If a page lights its own from a rumour of speed, the glass goes to smoke. One mean is all we need.— Willow Finch
