Sixteen Milliseconds

A small creed for wallets that tell the truth on time.

Every frame on a 60 Hz screen gets about sixteen milliseconds. Spend them on the thumb, not the ledger. Heavy work belongs off the UI thread, in slices small enough that a scroll never stutters while the wallet catches up.

And while it catches up, say so plainly: Catching up: block X of tip Y. No spinner that means nothing and no balance that pretends. A receipt the chain has already buried deep enough should move to Spendable as soon as the wallet sees it, not when a whole pass finishes. Speed and honesty turn out to be the same job.

Sixteen milliseconds to a frame; the thumb moves first, the ledger takes no blame. Say "block X of Y", not "please wait"; a number shows how far, a spinner hides how late. Promote the coin the moment it is sure, and keep the paint smooth while the scans endure.— Felix Uxbridge

Tidemark

A height chip that can't lie, drawn as a harbour wall.

The water level is your local sealed height. A thin tidemark on the wall is the highest tip any node peer has told you about, and the wet band between them is how far behind you are. No band means you're level. The tidemark only moves when a node says so: the pool can shout from the pier, but it can't paint the wall.

If no peer has spoken for a while, the tidemark slowly fades from ink to pencil, so a stale reading looks stale. Pending notes float as little boats. They bob from 0 conf, and at 9 they tie up at the dock as Spendable.

The pool calls out the height of the sea; the wall keeps only what the nodes agree. A pencil line means "I haven't heard"; ink means a peer has given its word. Boats bob in at zero, tie up at nine. Nothing on this wall is drawn by design.— Felix Uxbridge
