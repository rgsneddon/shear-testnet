One Purse, Then Many

A small fable about a batch that forgot its headers.

Once there was a pool whose share batch lost its paperwork on the way to the mint. Every share still did its work, but without a header to prove it, the batch looked empty, and the whole pot rolled into one purse, whichever one happened to stand first in line.

Nobody stole anything. The ledger simply believed what it was told, and what it was told was nothing. The fix was not a bigger lock. It was keeping the receipts attached, so every share that did the work could show it at the gate and get its own fair cut.

A pot with no paperwork pours to one cup, the first in the queue gets to drink it all up. Pin every receipt to the share that it paid, and the pot finds the many hands by which it was made.— Owen Ledger

Quiet Coin

The operator's purse that never jingles in public.

A good custodian is boring on purpose. Quiet Coin is the operator wallet as a closed purse on a felt table: fee destination, payout batches, and admin boundaries all present and accounted for, but nothing rattles unless an authorised hand opens it.

Every payout leaves a soft felt mark showing the amount, the height, and the tx. Every attempted change to the fee destination sounds one sharp clink and waits for a second key. When nothing's happening the purse makes no sound at all, and that silence is how you know custody is working.

No jingle, no flash, no coin on the floor. A purse on the felt, and a lock on the door. The only sound is the clink of a key, and the ledger writes down what it saw. Honestly.— Owen Ledger
