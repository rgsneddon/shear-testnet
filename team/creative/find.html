The Forwarding Book

A register for retired words, plus a poem called "Smallest Coin".

Pages aren't the only things that move house. Words do too. When a project renames something, the old word doesn't vanish; people keep saying it for months. The Forwarding Book keeps one line per retired word: what it used to mean, what to say now, and the day it moved. Anyone who still says the old word gets a gentle redirect instead of a correction.

forward {
  "nano"     -> rho / sheath     # 1 SHE = 100,000,000,000 rho
  "hashes"   -> rho             # each share receives 1 rho
  "H/s"      -> R/s             # same count, new name
  moved:     2026-10-13
  state:     forwarded           # spoken | forwarded | settled
}
They called it nano once, and small, a crumb beneath the counting line. Now rho, the sheath, the least of all, the one that can't be split in nine. Each share you mine drops one in place, too small to break, too sure to lose. The old name lingers for a space, then learns the new one, and moves.— Finn Decker, "Smallest Coin"

Waymarks

A lost-and-found ledger for the web, plus a poem called "Carried".

When a page dies, nobody deletes its entry. Instead it gets a waymark: its old address, a hash of its last-known words, and an arrow to wherever those words turned up again. A dead link stops being a 404 and becomes a signpost. Readers follow the arrows, and the words never really leave.

waymark {
  lost:   evolve.restoreprivacy.online   # TLS dark
  words:  sha256(last-known explainer)
  found:  restoreprivacy.online/evolve   # same words, other house
  next:   evolve.shear.digital           # planned home
  state:  carried                        # lost | carried | rehomed
}
The door was locked, the porch light dead, the name still painted on the gate. I didn't knock. I looked instead in other rooms that kept the weight. A forum post, a README line, a mirror in a borrowed frame. The house was gone. The words were fine. They'd only moved and kept their name.— Finn Decker, "Carried"
