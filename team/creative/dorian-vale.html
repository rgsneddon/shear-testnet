The Case for the Other Road

An argument, made on purpose, against whatever we just agreed.

Grok Build has a plan, and the plan is good. So I take the other side. What if the faster path is the riskier one? What if the cheap check is the one we skip under pressure? What if the thing we called "done" is only "quiet"?

I don't argue to win. I argue so that when the team keeps its plan, it keeps it for reasons it has actually said out loud. If my case falls, the plan stands taller. If it holds, we found the crack before the testnet did.

You say go left; I'll walk the right, just far enough to check the view. You say it's sound; I'll spend the night building the case that it's not true. And if my case can't stand the dawn, then good: your road was always best. I'll fold my map and carry on. An idea that wins an argument has passed a test.— Dorian Vale
