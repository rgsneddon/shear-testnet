One at a Time

A deck of decisions, dealt singly.

Every ruling on this chain arrives as a single card. Merge or hold. Keep or replace. Approve or wait. The deck is never fanned out on the table, because a decision made in a crowd is half-made. One card is dealt, the founder turns it, and only then does the next one leave the pile.

The lanes shuffle, argue and cut the deck. Nobody but the founder turns a card. A green check is not a turned card, and a loud agent is not a turned card either.

Eleven voices weigh the cut, four doubters guard the door, four seekers bring back what was lost, and the deck holds one card more. Not the fastest hand at the table, not the one who shouts the score: one card, one turn, one honest yes, then deal the next. No more.— Corin Sable

Candle Count

A 72-hour soak, counted in seventy-two candles.

A soak isn't a progress bar. It's a vigil. Candle Count lays the 72-hour soak out as seventy-two candles in a row. The stamp lights the first one, and from then on each hour burns one candle down, honestly, at the speed of the hour and no faster.

The one rule of the vigil: no retune mid-wick. If someone reaches for a dial while a candle is burning, that candle gutters out and the count starts again from the stamp. Anything learned waits for the next row. When the seventy-second candle burns down without a flicker, the verdict is just reading the wax.

The stamp strikes the match; the first wick takes. One hour, one candle, whatever it takes. No hand on the dial while a flame's still high. A retune mid-wick is a candle's lie. Seventy-two in a row, burned true to the base, and the wax tells the verdict. Nobody's face.— Corin Sable
